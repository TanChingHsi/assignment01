So: why are we here?

#Why are we here anyway?

Code is the language of digital media, which dominates a lot of modern media of the current time. Lots of the more attractive forms of media actually rely on being interactive, in a way, similar to old styles of entertainment such as stand-up comedy and improv shows, where humans interact with the media, and are involved and entertained by the output of their input. Ultimately, interaction is what computers do best, and its what code is often for. Although generative AI can create almost perfect code off of one singular sentence, it isnt enough. Why? Because automation lacks clear intent. It is ultimately still a machine, interpreting letters on a user's input to generate the most likely result that'd satisfy the user. The generative part of the AI is really just another very complicated piece of code that generates code. So, why do we care? Because a designer is supposed to be meticulous, and know precisely what needs to change in order to achieve a satisfying result. It goes for artists too, to no lesser extent. We are supposed to have a vision as artists, designers or even technitians. It's a precise way of dealing with a known issue as a technitian or a very specific, visually intended and pleasing result in a design, or a very human vision for what essentially is another medium for artists to express themselves. Therefore it's still important to be able to interperet your own code, even if AI can do the heavy lifting. Software engineering can be a means to an end, but it's an art form and very beneficial to have a elegant way of getting to your goal. For example as a designer, you'd want your userbase to actually feel satisfied with whatever you're making so you'd wanna be a bit of a perfectionist. By learning about the general gist of programming and being able to read programs, it'd be much easier to point out and work with AI to reduce redundant parts of the code that can make your own code become more explict, to be easier understood by your techncian colleagues. To be good with code also means sometimes you'd know a better solution than the algorithm and can specify things such as what kind of sorting algorithm suits your purpose. (e.g. Interpolation Search for a name with a laaaaaarge database of past artworks) And to be very direct with how the course is designed and to be less philosophical and out there... it's still extremely important to learn industry skills such as utilizing Github, being able to explain what you're doing with your code to your collaborators and to have a firsthand experience in co-operation in an environment with heavy technical work, as in the digital age, lots of media and art that involves entertainment would be... well, digital. It's incredibly obvious and actually pointed out with great detail in the video "the Art of Coding" where coding can just be the process of creating art, so even for artists, it is actually a form of media that can be used to express themselves in many ways, from algorithms that draw, to computer animations and important leaps in computer graphics, emerging and maturing mediums such as games and 3D interactable media... Things like voxels, meshes and textures can be placed with procedually generators, along with 'knobs'to mess with the variables, seed, and generation perameters of the result. There is really an advantage to be had, beyond technical effieciency and perfection, to be able to understand and adjust the generation results or design a certain 'feel' of a product by knowing and communicating with an agent to (or even manually) improving upon or adujusting existing code is an amazing skillset, and that is why even as designers and artists, it'd be important for us to learn the very nessicary skill to interperet, improve, explain, and share code while being able to appreciate the coding language and algorithmic logic as the elegant art pieces of past human genius they are, in the same way people can understand and appreciate wine, and the process of making fine wine, but only if they study the process and learn to interpret the history and logic behind the amazing results the product can achieve, even when the tedious wine making (and in this case code-generating) isn't done by our own hands. That is why I think we're here for, because coding itself is an art form and an extention of the designer since it is a result of their input even if the code is generated.
